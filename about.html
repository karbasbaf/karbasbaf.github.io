A Biography in Resonance

Two chapters, one continuous line of inquiry — from a workbench to a research desk, the instrument changes but the question doesn't: where does a wave decide to speak, and what shape is it asking to be given?

I

Foundation in Craft

The work begins at the workbench, not the desk. Years spent in hands-on contact with stringed instruments — investigating, building, and repairing traditional Persian lutes — meant learning first through the hands, and only later through measurement: why one soundhole speaks and another sits mute, why a maker's inherited instinct for proportion so often lands exactly where the physics would have predicted, if anyone had thought to ask it the question first.

That inherited instinct became the starting point for the Golden Method — an attempt to make explicit the acoustic wisdom already embedded, wordlessly, in centuries of craftsmanship, and to give soundhole design a geometry that could be taught rather than only inherited. The instrument stayed the same. What changed was the willingness to ask a craft tradition to explain itself in the language of resonance.

II

Expansion of Wave Analysis

The same question — where does a wave decide to speak? — turned out not to belong to instruments alone. Extending that acoustic lens to Persian architectural heritage revealed wave-analysis principles quietly operating within structural ornamental elements long assumed to be purely decorative: the tong bori, the muqarnas vault, the perforated lattice, each one behaving, under analysis, less like ornament and more like an instrument that had simply never been asked to perform.

Current research follows that same principle further outward still — toward electromagnetic and plasma wave phenomena in astrophysical and nebular environments, where resonance operates at a scale no workbench could ever hold, but where the underlying question hasn't changed at all: given a cavity, a boundary, a medium capable of oscillation, what shape does the wave ask to be given, and what is listening for the answer.
